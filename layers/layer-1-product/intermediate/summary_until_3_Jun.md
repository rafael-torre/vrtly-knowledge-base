## 1. What Vrtly Does

Vrtly is a **point-of-care (POC) advertising platform for medical aesthetics**. It connects aesthetic brands (advertisers) with medical practices (venues) via an AI-powered ad network delivered through Smart TV apps installed on practice waiting room and treatment room screens.

Self-described as the **"All-in-One Media Network"** for medical aesthetics. Core tagline: *"The Point of Care is the Point of Decision."*

**The business logic:** brands spend heavily on Meta and Google to build awareness but lose the consumer at the moment that matters most — when they are physically inside a clinic. Vrtly closes this loop. 80% of treatment decisions happen in-clinic. Average patient dwell time is 45–90 minutes. 65% of new treatment conversations are prompted at the POC. 70% of patients are interested in new procedures, but 7 in 10 do not have a specific brand in mind at point of care.

**Current scale:**
- ~**2,000 active practices** (mostly single-location owner-operators: med spas, dermatology, plastic surgery)
- ~**3,000+ active connected screens** deployed (2.5 screens per practice on average)
- **30M monthly impressions**
- **281 live brands**; **3,000+ brand content items** in library
- **20 live ad campaigns** (since Q3 2024 launch)
- Growth is intentionally paused: focus right now is **reliability before scale**; target is 10,000+ practices

## 6. How the Platform Works — Technical Architecture

### Content flow
1. Practice or brand uploads MP4 via web portal
2. FFmpeg transcodes to HLS → stored in S3 → served via CloudFront
3. Playlists **generated daily per screen** server-side and pushed to devices over WebSocket
4. Devices **cache playlists locally** and play from cache — not a live stream
5. In degraded network: playback continues from cache; watchdog escalates: skip → reset playlist → full app reset

### Web portals
- **VPM** — practice-facing portal (Vue 3 / TypeScript monorepo)
- **VAM** — brand/advertiser-facing portal (Vue 3 / TypeScript monorepo)

### Backend (Java / Spring — four services)
| Service | Role |
|---|---|
| `player-api` | Device connections and content streaming |
| `reach-n-freq` | Playlist generation and video transcoding |
| `api` | Main monolith — billing and content upload |
| `fm-common` | Shared library across all services |

- **MySQL on RDS** — system of record
- **Elasticsearch** — 100M+ telemetry and analytics events
- **Kibana** — ad-hoc dashboard visualization on top of Elasticsearch
- **WebSocket** — real-time channel between devices and backend; also carries telemetry

### Device fleet

| Runtime | Platforms covered | Fleet share | Audit scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| `html5core-player` | Samsung Tizen, LG WebOS, Fire TV, iOS WebView | ~50% of fleet | **In scope — Phase 1** |
| Roku player | RokuOS | — | **In scope — Phase 1** |
| Android / ExoPlayer | Google TV, external HDMI device | — | **In scope — Phase 1** |
| Apple TV | Native Swift | — | Lower priority |
| iOS (iPad / iPhone) | Native | — | Phasing out |
| Legacy external hardware | — | ~50% of fleet | Phasing out over 6 months |

- ~600 different device types across the fleet
- Anecdotally: older Android hardware and Samsung TVs (certain model groups) generate most complaints

## 7. Current Reliability State — The Core Problem

### Blank screens are the central business failure
- ~47–50 distinct failure causes catalogued across content encoding, network delivery, device hardware, app code, and platform OS behavior
- Current telemetry correctly classifies approximately **4% of those failure causes**
- ~48 of 50 known failure types are **invisible in production today**
- **No proactive alerting** — problems discovered when customers call or silently churn

### Telemetry architecture gaps
- All device telemetry flows over **WebSocket → Elasticsearch → Kibana**
- When WebSocket closes, visibility ends — team cannot see what happened before or during disconnect
- Team knows *what content was playing* at disconnect but **not why** (network, crash, content fetch failure, etc.)
- Joe: *"we don't know what we don't know"*
- No Grafana, no Prometheus, no structured alerting layer
- **Roku telemetry has been off since March** — entire platform is completely dark

### What does exist and works
- **Canary deploy capability exists** — can modulate which server an app pulls from at runtime
- **Feature flags by org ID** — new experiences can be scoped to specific practices
- **Device classification script** — user agent scraped, classified via Claude Haiku, enriched via nightly Google search; **96–98% accurate**
- Elasticsearch + WebSocket infrastructure described by Joe as *"good and robust"* — the problem is not the pipeline, it is knowing **what data to put in it**

### Active firefight — VT-5083
- Active multi-service incident spanning `player-api`, `reach-n-freq`, and `fm-common`
- m3u8 fixes, quarantine logic, quality tier-fall, GOP duration changes mid-rollout
- Same fixes being committed twice within days
- **Credentials found committed in two repos** and not yet fully rotated
- No resolution timeline confirmed

### Other production posture gaps
- No circuit breakers on the playback watchdog
- No server-side rate limiting on WebSocket reconnects
- No external alerting
- A bad merge to the `html5core` prod branch poisons the entire Samsung/LG/FireTV fleet within **15 minutes with no rollback path**

## 10. How Problems Are Reported

- Practices call or email Customer Support — **no self-service portal** for device issues
- CS has a telemetry dashboard but it is incomplete and hard to use; most issues escalate to engineering
- **No inbound signal from churned or silent users** — if a practice stops using the product, Vrtly does not know

### Activation funnel impact (David, Product Designer)
- Activation is already hard: acquire a screen, set it up, activate it
- When a screen goes blank after activation, users lose trust immediately
- Most do not reach out to support — they simply stop using the product (especially on the free tier)
- Quantifying churn caused by blank screens is **currently impossible**

## 12. The Mitigation Plan

**Phase 1 — Close the telemetry gap**
- Correctly detect and classify ≥80% of known failure causes within one month
- Verified by a synthetic QA test suite run against physical devices
- Three parallel workstreams: html5core-player, Roku, Android/ExoPlayer
- All feeding a standardized event envelope into Elasticsearch
- First unlock: **re-enable Roku telemetry** (completely blind since March)
- 80% target = implementing the standard QoE signals that cover dominant failure mechanisms — not 80% of devices

**Phase 2 — Drive blank screen rate below 0.1%**
- Uses Phase 1 telemetry data to drive resolution
- **Phase 2 does not start until Phase 1 is trustworthy**

**KPIs:**
- Blank screen rate below threshold
- Telemetry coverage above 80%
- Zero production incidents per week
- Specific features shipped

**Joe's sequencing strategy (Netflix reference):**
- Joe wrote a technical doc modeled on Netflix's QoE research blog
- Implement standard signals → get true positive blank-screen events in lab → release to production → use telemetry fingerprints to confirm patterns in the wild

## 13. Technology Evaluations in Progress

### Mux Video (replacing FFmpeg/HLS transcoding)
- Current FFmpeg pipeline has **silent failure modes** — content can finish "transcoded" with missing variants, nothing downstream notices
- Mux Video: managed pipeline with reliable encoding, storage, and multi-CDN delivery
- Migration tooling pulls directly from existing S3 library
- ~40k videos averaging 60 seconds → storage cost ~**$220/month**
- **Verdict: straightforward yes**

### Mux Data (QoE analytics)
- SDKs for every Vrtly runtime: HLS.js, ExoPlayer, AVPlayer, Roku, Tizen
- Supports custom dimensions (`screen_id`, `org_id`) alongside standard QoE metrics
- **Pricing problem**: charges per video view → ~36M views/month at current scale in a looping signage context
- In conversation with Mux about **device-based pricing** before committing

### Sentry (resilient event layer)
- Joe responded positively when raised — Sentry can **cache events and handle callbacks even when the WebSocket drops**
- Directly solves the core visibility gap at disconnect
- Natural recommendation: Sentry (or equivalent) as a resilient event layer on top of or replacing WebSocket-only telemetry

### Grafana
- Raised as a recommendation; Joe did not push back
- Currently absent from the stack
- Natural candidate as the visualization and alerting layer over existing Elasticsearch data
