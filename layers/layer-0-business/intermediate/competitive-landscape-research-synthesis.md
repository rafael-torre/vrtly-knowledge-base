# Research Synthesis — Competitive Landscape

**Subject:** Surface-level research on Vrtly's four named direct competitors: PatientPoint, TouchMD, LVBX, and CheckedUp.

**Sources:** Public web sources (company websites, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, Growjo, LeadIQ, Inc. 5000, press releases, trade publications) researched June 2, 2026. No proprietary or paid data was used.

**Method:** Four parallel AI-assisted web research tasks, one per competitor. Each task focused on: product offering, markets served, business model, cost-to-practice, hardware/content, company size/ownership, and relative strengths and weaknesses vs. Vrtly. Results synthesized into this document, organized to map to the sections of `final/competitive-landscape.md`.

---

## Summary

All four named competitors operate in the broader point-of-care (POC) digital media and patient engagement space, but Vrtly is the only one purpose-built as an advertising marketplace specifically for medical aesthetics. The competitive landscape divides into two clear types:

**Broad healthcare POC networks (PatientPoint, CheckedUp):** Large, PE-backed, pharma-funded networks that serve many specialties. Free to practices (brand/pharma-funded model). Much greater scale than Vrtly today, but not aesthetics-native — their advertisers are pharma and life sciences brands, not aesthetic device/injectable brands. Both are now investing in programmatic advertising infrastructure.

**Aesthetics-native workflow and marketing tools (TouchMD, LVBX):** Built specifically for the aesthetics vertical with deep practice relationships. However, both charge practices a subscription fee — the opposite of Vrtly's free model. Neither operates a true programmatic ad marketplace; brand content is delivered as a bundled content library or direct partnership, not as a measured media buy with AI matching.

Vrtly's differentiated position — free to practices, practice controls which brands appear, instant activation on existing hardware, purpose-built for aesthetics — is distinct from all four competitors. The primary competitive risks are (a) **incumbency**: TouchMD is already embedded in 16,000+ aesthetic practices; (b) **expansion threat**: PatientPoint and CheckedUp have the infrastructure to enter aesthetics more deliberately if they choose.

## Key Findings

### PatientPoint

**What they are:** The largest POC digital media network in the US. Founded 1987, Cincinnati OH. ~30,000 physician offices, 125,000+ providers, 350M+ annual adult visits. Acquired by Advent International PE in Q4 2025. Estimated $500M+ annual revenue.

**Business model:** Brands (primarily pharma/Rx) pay to advertise; practices receive hardware and service at no cost. Secondary streams: SaaS for health systems, anonymized audience data (claims-level Rx/ICD-10 targeting) sold to pharma. Custom pricing — no public rate card.

**Product:** Waiting room TVs, exam room touchscreens (Interact), back-office clinical news (Access), hospital navigation (Navigate). All on proprietary PatientPoint hardware — not the practice's existing TVs. In March 2026, launched **Health Audiences** (programmatic ad buying using claims data) and **Consumer Health Network** (extending beyond pharma to OTC/CPG/wellness brands).

**Markets:** Broad healthcare — oncology, cardiology, primary care, health systems, dermatology. No dedicated aesthetics/medspa vertical. Practices cannot granularly control which brands appear on their screens.

**Notable context:** Previously merged with Outcome Health (2021), whose co-founder was sentenced to 7.5 years for fraud in July 2024. PatientPoint was not implicated, but the reputational overhang exists.

**Strengths vs. Vrtly:** Dominant scale, 35+ years of brand trust, sophisticated data/targeting infrastructure, strong PE backing, multiple specialty society partnerships.

**Weaknesses vs. Vrtly:** Not aesthetics-native; practices have no brand control; requires proprietary hardware installation (higher friction); pharma advertiser base doesn't overlap with aesthetic brands; Outcome Health fraud legacy.

**Watch for the final doc:** The March 2026 Consumer Health Network launch is the most relevant near-term threat — if PatientPoint begins actively recruiting aesthetic brands as CPG/wellness advertisers, it could start competing for Vrtly's core advertiser relationships even without a dedicated aesthetics vertical.

### TouchMD

**What they are:** A patient engagement and consultation SaaS platform built for medical aesthetics. Founded 2009, Cedar City UT. Acquired by Nextech in October 2022; Nextech acquired by TPG Capital for $1.4B in July 2023. ~16,000+ aesthetic professionals on the platform.

**Business model:** Practices pay a monthly subscription (custom pricing; Playlist module alone valued at ~$1,200/year). Brands participate via a **Preferred Vendor Program** — they pay TouchMD to place content in the platform's content library and Playlist screens. No public pricing for brand partnerships.

**Product:** Core suite is consultation (TouchMD Consult on iPad), clinical photography (Snap Pro), digital consents, telehealth (Reach), push notifications (Blast), and a patient app (myTouchMD). **TouchMD Playlist** is the in-office screen product — a customizable video loop on any Smart TV or Amazon Fire Stick. Practices supply their own hardware; TouchMD does not provide it for free.

**Markets:** Medical aesthetics (plastic surgery, medspas, derm) primarily, with ophthalmology and women's health as secondary verticals.

**Notable context:** Playlist is included "free" with other paid TouchMD modules — it is bundled secondary functionality, not the flagship. AmSpa members get Playlist free. The parent company Nextech has ~250–500 employees and integrates with 30+ practice management systems.

**Strengths vs. Vrtly:** Deep workflow integration (the iPad in the provider's hand during consultation); 16,000+ aesthetic professionals — strongest incumbency of all four; full product suite creates stickiness; EMR integrations; institutional PE backing; trusted in aesthetics (AmSpa Platinum Vendor).

**Weaknesses vs. Vrtly:** Practices pay; no free hardware; advertising is bundled add-on, not a purpose-built ad network; no evidence of AI matching or programmatic capability; small dedicated team (~32 people within Nextech); mixed patient app ratings (3.6–3.9/5).

**Watch for the final doc:** TouchMD's incumbency in aesthetic practices is the most operationally significant competitive challenge for Vrtly — not because TouchMD is a better ad product, but because practices already running TouchMD Playlist may need a clear reason to add or switch to Vrtly screens.

### LVBX (The Live Box Network)

**What they are:** A multi-channel content marketing and distribution platform for medical aesthetics and beauty practices. Founded 2009, Newport Beach CA. Privately held, bootstrapped. ~2 employees, ~$2.7M annual revenue (LinkedIn estimates).

**Business model:** Practices pay a direct subscription — $99/month per module (Video, Social, Newsletter, Text Connect). Some brands may subsidize the cost for partner practices, but this is not the primary model. No free tier.

**Product:** Four modules — **LVBX Video** (in-practice digital signage via proprietary hardware device, $59/unit), **LVBX Social** (social media scheduling with aesthetics content library), **LVBX Newsletter** (automated patient email), **LVBX Text Connect** (SMS marketing). Content mix includes brand videos, lifestyle content (fashion, travel), and custom practice uploads. Hardware is proprietary (connects to any HDTV via cable); managed remotely via "LVBX Producer" cloud software.

**Markets:** Exclusively aesthetics and beauty — medspas, plastic surgery, derm, salons.

**Notable context:** Website and public presence appear largely static since 2020–2022. No recent press releases, product launches, or funding announcements found. One former LVBX employee moved to Vrtly — confirming overlap in talent pool.

**Strengths vs. Vrtly:** Broader channel coverage (social + email + SMS + screens in one tool); 15+ years of aesthetic brand relationships; content richness (lifestyle alongside brand video); social media management is a differentiated value prop.

**Weaknesses vs. Vrtly:** Practices pay (vs. Vrtly free model); tiny team with limited scalability; no programmatic ad network — brand content is curation, not a measured media buy; no AI matching or attribution; hardware dependency; dated product investment.

**Watch for the final doc:** LVBX is the smallest and least threatening competitor — but their brand content relationships and ~2,000 practice footprint are relevant for the "existing alternatives" framing in the final document. Their $99/month pricing point also sets a reference for what practices are currently accustomed to paying.

### CheckedUp

**What they are:** A physician-founded POC digital media and patient education company. Founded 2012, New York NY. PE-backed by Rockbridge Growth Equity. ~111–113 employees. Named to Inc. 5000 four times; 268% three-year revenue growth (most credible public signal — actual revenue undisclosed, estimated $16M–$100M range from third-party aggregators).

**Business model:** Two-sided media network — free to practices, pharma/life sciences brands pay for ad inventory. In May 2025, launched **IAB-compliant programmatic advertising**, enabling media agencies to activate campaigns across their network via standard DSP/SSP pipelines. Monthly "Proof of Play" reporting to advertisers.

**Product:** Two hardware products provided free to practices — **Explorer** (waiting room TV) and **Consult** (interactive exam room wallboard). Content includes health education, medical association content, original TV programming (two shows launched 2025), and sponsored brand ads. Content appears to be centrally managed by CheckedUp; no evidence that practices can select which specific brands appear.

**Markets:** Broad multi-specialty healthcare — ophthalmology (founding vertical), rheumatology, dermatology, cardiology, neurology, women's health. Partner of the **American Society for Dermatologic Surgery (ASDS)**, which gives presence in dermatology/aesthetics — but aesthetics is one slice of a much wider network. Core advertiser relationships are pharma/life sciences, not aesthetic brands.

**Notable context:** Appeared at IAB NewFronts 2025 alongside major media companies — signals intent to be taken seriously as a media network, not just a healthtech tool. Acquired Health Media Network in 2022. New CMO hired late 2025 (from GoodRx, DermTech background).

**Strengths vs. Vrtly:** Significant scale (30,000+ HCPs), fast-growing (40% YoY revenue growth), programmatic infrastructure, ASDS partnership, pharma-grade measurement, PE-backed, original content.

**Weaknesses vs. Vrtly:** Not aesthetics-native — their advertiser base is pharma/life sciences, not the aesthetics brand ecosystem; practices don't appear to control which brands appear; exam-room wallboard requires dedicated hardware installation; no AI matching mentioned.

**Watch for the final doc:** CheckedUp is structurally the closest analog to what Vrtly is building (free screens, brand-funded, two-sided network) — but operates at a different healthcare layer. If CheckedUp expands its ASDS relationship into a full aesthetics vertical push, it would become the most formidable direct competitor given its scale, PE backing, and now-programmatic ad infrastructure.

## Implications for `final/competitive-landscape.md`

### Market Position section

The research supports framing Vrtly as a **niche challenger** in a nascent category — POC digital advertising for medical aesthetics. The broader POC media market has established players (PatientPoint at $500M+ revenue), but no competitor is purpose-built for the aesthetics vertical with Vrtly's model (brand-funded free screens + practice brand control + programmatic marketplace). Vrtly is best described as a **category creator** within medical aesthetics POC advertising, not a challenger to any existing dominant player in that specific niche.

### Key Competitors section

The comparison table in the final doc should capture the five dimensions Vrtly uses in its own pitch deck positioning:

| Dimension | PatientPoint | TouchMD | LVBX | CheckedUp | Vrtly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Practice controls which brands it advertises | ✗ | Partial (content library opt-in) | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ |
| Point-of-care delivery | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Content library of aesthetics brands | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |
| Free for practices | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Instant activation, no additional hardware | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ |

Research confirms Vrtly's self-reported positioning is accurate. The "practice controls which brands it advertises" and "instant activation, no additional hardware" claims are not replicated by any named competitor.

### Client Differentiators section

Four research-backed differentiators are supported:

1. **Aesthetics-native advertiser relationships**: None of the four competitors have a primary advertiser base of aesthetic brands (injectables, devices, skincare). PatientPoint and CheckedUp serve pharma; LVBX and TouchMD have brand content libraries but not a measurable programmatic ad marketplace.
2. **Free-to-practice, brand-funded model with practice brand control**: CheckedUp and PatientPoint are also free to practices, but practices have no control over advertiser selection. TouchMD and LVBX charge practices. Vrtly is the only one combining free access with explicit practice-side brand control.
3. **Zero-friction hardware deployment**: All four competitors require some form of hardware purchase, installation, or proprietary device. Vrtly activates on existing Smart TVs instantly.
4. **Purpose-built ad network with AI matching**: None of the four run a true two-sided programmatic marketplace for aesthetics. TouchMD's Preferred Vendor Program and LVBX's brand content are direct-sales relationships. PatientPoint and CheckedUp are building programmatic but for pharma audiences.

### Industry Trends section

The research surfaces several trends worth including:

- **Programmatic POC advertising is emerging**: PatientPoint (March 2026) and CheckedUp (May 2025) both launched programmatic ad-buying infrastructure for their networks. This signals the category is maturing toward agency-accessible, measurable media buys — and sets expectations for what Vrtly will need to offer as it scales.
- **PE consolidation in healthcare IT**: Nextech/TouchMD is owned by TPG ($1.4B); PatientPoint is owned by Advent International; CheckedUp is backed by Rockbridge. The space is attracting institutional capital — validating the market but also raising the competitive bar for technology and scale.
- **Consumer health brands entering POC**: PatientPoint's March 2026 Consumer Health Network explicitly opens its network to OTC, CPG, and wellness brands. This is a signal that the boundary between pharma POC advertising and consumer aesthetics advertising is blurring.
- **Privacy changes driving POC investment**: Loss of third-party cookies and mobile tracking (referenced in Vrtly's own pitch deck) is pushing brands to invest in first-party, contextually relevant channels — of which POC is one. This macro tailwind benefits the entire category.
- **Content differentiation as a moat**: CheckedUp launched two original TV shows in 2025; PatientPoint partners with AAD and NCCN for credentialed educational content. Content quality and medical credibility are emerging as competitive levers alongside ad targeting.

## Gaps and Limitations

- **No direct product evaluations**: All findings are based on public-facing marketing, websites, and press materials. Actual product capabilities (especially targeting, reporting, and AI features) may differ from what is marketed.
- **Revenue figures are third-party estimates** for private companies (especially CheckedUp and LVBX): treat ranges as directional, not precise.
- **TouchMD Preferred Vendor Program pricing**: Not publicly available — the economics of how brands pay for content placement through TouchMD are unclear from public sources.
- **LVBX brand partnership details**: The claim that "some brands may subsidize LVBX for practices" was found but not substantiated. The extent of this program is unknown.
- **Practice control claims**: For PatientPoint and CheckedUp, the absence of practice brand-control claims in public materials is meaningful — but absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. A direct product evaluation would be needed to confirm.
- **Vrtly's own competitive claims not independently verified**: This synthesis takes Vrtly's pitch deck self-assessment as a starting point; it has not been independently verified against competitor products.
- **No aesthetics industry analyst reports found**: No third-party analyst (e.g., KLAS, Gartner, AmSpa research) covering this specific niche was identified. The competitive landscape framing is based on company-level public research, not industry-level benchmarking.
