# Competitive Landscape

Where Vrtly sits in their market, who they compete with, and what forces are shaping their industry. This is a research-backed reference document — consulted during ideation, positioning discussions, and strategic decisions, not a daily driver.

*Source: [Competitive Landscape Research Synthesis](../intermediate/competitive-landscape-research-synthesis.md) — public web research, June 2, 2026.*

## Market Position

Vrtly operates in the emerging category of **point-of-care (POC) digital advertising for medical aesthetics** — a niche within a broader healthcare media market that includes large established networks like PatientPoint ($500M+ revenue, 350M+ annual patient visits). Within that broader market, Vrtly is best described as a **category creator**: no competitor is purpose-built for the aesthetics vertical with Vrtly's specific model — brand-funded free screens, practice-side brand control, and a programmatic ad marketplace.

The broader POC digital media space is dominated by pharma-funded, multi-specialty networks. Aesthetics-specific tools exist (TouchMD, LVBX), but they charge practices a subscription and do not operate as true ad marketplaces. Vrtly's category is nascent — which means low direct competition in the core niche, but also that Vrtly must validate the model while larger players (PatientPoint, CheckedUp) build the infrastructure to potentially enter it.

## Key Competitors

The competitive landscape divides into two types: **broad healthcare POC networks** that are large, pharma-funded, and not aesthetics-native; and **aesthetics-native workflow tools** that are deeply embedded in practices but charge subscription fees and don't operate ad networks.

**Broad healthcare POC networks (PatientPoint, CheckedUp)** are free to practices and funded by brand/pharma advertising — structurally similar to Vrtly — but their advertiser base is pharma and life sciences, not aesthetic brands. Neither has a dedicated aesthetics vertical. Both are now building programmatic ad infrastructure (PatientPoint in March 2026, CheckedUp in May 2025), which raises the eventual competitive bar. CheckedUp (via its ASDS partnership) has the clearest path to entering aesthetics if it chooses to pursue it deliberately.

**Aesthetics-native tools (TouchMD, LVBX)** have deep practice relationships and brand content libraries within the aesthetics vertical, but operate on the opposite business model — practices pay a monthly subscription, and brand content is delivered as a curated library rather than a measured media buy. TouchMD's incumbency is the most operationally significant challenge for Vrtly: with 16,000+ aesthetic professionals already using TouchMD Playlist, practices need a clear reason to add or switch to Vrtly screens.

## Client Differentiators

Vrtly's competitive position rests on four research-confirmed advantages that no named competitor replicates in combination:

1. **Aesthetics-native advertiser relationships.** No competitor's primary advertiser base is aesthetic brands (injectables, devices, skincare). PatientPoint and CheckedUp serve pharma; LVBX and TouchMD have brand content libraries but not a programmatic ad marketplace where aesthetic brands buy measured media. Vrtly is building the only network where those brands are the core customer.

2. **Free to practices with practice-side brand control.** PatientPoint and CheckedUp are also free to practices, but practices have no control over which advertisers appear on their screens. LVBX and TouchMD charge practices. Vrtly is the only model that combines zero cost to practices with explicit practice control over which brands are shown — a meaningful trust and consent differentiator in a relationship-driven vertical.

3. **Zero-friction hardware deployment.** All four competitors require some form of hardware purchase, installation, or proprietary device. Vrtly activates instantly on any existing Smart TV in the practice — removing the biggest implementation barrier and shrinking the sales cycle.

4. **Purpose-built programmatic ad marketplace for aesthetics.** None of the four competitors run a two-sided programmatic marketplace for aesthetics brands. TouchMD and LVBX operate direct-sales content relationships. PatientPoint and CheckedUp are building programmatic, but for pharma audiences. Vrtly's AI matching and campaign measurement is differentiated within the aesthetics vertical specifically.

## Relevant Industry Trends

- **Programmatic POC advertising is maturing.** PatientPoint launched Health Audiences (programmatic ad buying using claims data) in March 2026; CheckedUp launched IAB-compliant programmatic in May 2025, appearing at IAB NewFronts alongside major media companies. The category is moving toward agency-accessible, measurable media buys — setting expectations for what Vrtly will need to offer as it scales.

- **PE consolidation is raising the competitive bar.** Nextech/TouchMD is backed by TPG ($1.4B); PatientPoint by Advent International; CheckedUp by Rockbridge Growth Equity. Institutional capital is flowing into the space, validating the market opportunity but also funding competitors' product and sales investment.

- **Consumer health brands are entering POC networks.** PatientPoint's March 2026 Consumer Health Network explicitly opens their inventory to OTC, CPG, and wellness brands — blurring the line between pharma POC advertising and consumer aesthetics advertising. This is the clearest near-term signal that existing large networks may start competing for Vrtly's core advertiser relationships.

- **Privacy changes are driving investment in contextual, first-party channels.** Loss of third-party cookies and mobile tracking is pushing brands toward channels with first-party, contextually relevant access to audiences. POC advertising — reaching patients in a relevant healthcare setting — benefits from this macro shift. This tailwind supports the entire category, including Vrtly's pitch to aesthetic brands.

- **Content quality and medical credibility are emerging as competitive moats.** CheckedUp launched two original TV shows in 2025; PatientPoint partners with AAD, NCCN, and specialty societies for credentialed educational content. As networks compete for practice adoption, the quality and relevance of non-ad content on the screens becomes a differentiation lever alongside ad targeting.
